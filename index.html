Viriditas Alchemy

The sacred wisdom of Viriditas did not remain confined within the monastery of Rupertsberg. Hildegard of Bingen became a guide and inspiration to many remarkable souls of her time: from the great Saint Bernard of Clairvaux, who recognized her prophetic gift, to Guibert of Gembloux, her loyal disciple and secretary, and even Emperor Frederick Barbarossa, who sought her counsel.

Through the centuries this vital force continued to endure, reaching, according to the studies of Dr. Eleazar Majors, even the elites of the twentieth century, who sought renewed physical endurance and spiritual strength through the regenerative power of Hildegard's Viriditas.

Who Was Hildegard of Bingen?

Born in 1098 into a noble German family and passing away in 1179, Hildegard of Bingen is regarded as one of the most extraordinary and luminous figures of the Christian Middle Ages.

A Benedictine abbess, mystic, visionary, composer, scientist, physician, poet, and philosopher, she is universally recognized as one of the greatest polymaths of her era and one of the most influential figures in Western mysticism.

From childhood she experienced divine visions of a living light (umbra viventis lucis), which she described as the very energy of God permeating creation. She called this force Viriditas, the "greening power of life," an energy capable of bringing forth growth in nature, supporting the human body, and renewing the spirit.

The Tradition of Viriditas

The visions and wisdom of Viriditas did not disappear with the death of Hildegard of Bingen. Her faithful secretary and disciple Volmar, who had spent decades recording her revelations, preserved many of her teachings for future generations.

According to the studies of Dr. Eleazar Majors, this knowledge continued its journey through the centuries, crossing different eras and schools of thought while preserving the ancient quest for harmony between vital energy, physical well-being, and spiritual growth.

Today, Viriditas Alchemy draws inspiration from this tradition and from Hildegard's vision of vital force, embracing an approach that unites nature, knowledge, and well-being.
